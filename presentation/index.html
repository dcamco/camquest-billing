Proposal · November 2026

Sell hours. Burn hours. Pay techs. Close the books — automatically.

A time-block billing and payroll system for Camquest Systems: prepaid support blocks that deduct as techs log time, techs paid per quarter hour billed, QuickBooks Online kept in step without a bookkeeper, and a client portal that shows every hour and every invoice.

Today

The business model is simple. The bookkeeping around it isn't.

Clients buy 20- or 40-hour blocks. Techs send in their time. Someone subtracts it from the client, adds it to the tech's pay, and keys it all into QuickBooks Desktop — by hand, after the fact.

LagClients learn their balance when someone gets around to it; techs learn their pay at payday.
LeakageWork done at 7 AM from an RMM alert often never becomes a billed quarter hour.
DisputesMonth-end questions — "what was that 2.5 hours?" — with no notes to point at.
DependencyOne person holds the process in their head and in a spreadsheet. The goal is to retire that role.
ChasingPast-due invoices get noticed when cash is tight, not when they go past due.
DesktopQuickBooks Desktop can't be integrated with cleanly; QuickBooks Online can.
The idea

One ledger. Every approved quarter hour writes three postings at once.

The client's block goes down, the tech's pay goes up, and QuickBooks gets the record — from a single approval. No re-keying, no spreadsheet, no drift between what the client was told and what the books say.

For techs

Thirty seconds on a phone. The math is shown before you submit.

  • Date, client, device (pulled from N-sight), on-site or remote, what you did
  • Quarter-hour rounding and the 1-hour on-site / 15-minute remote minimums applied visibly
  • "From RMM: GULF-DC01 backup check failed and cleared — was that you?" one tap to log it
  • My pay: earned this period, awaiting approval, prior deposits — no more waiting for payday to know
  • Edit until approved; a rejected entry comes back with a reason

For the owner

One weekly approval, one monthly close, and nothing to re-key.

Approvals

Every submitted entry in one queue with billed hours, block remaining and pay effect. Approve all, or send one back with a reason. Entries that would overdraft a block are flagged by the rule you choose.

Receivables

Aging buckets, every open invoice with its status, a red badge the moment anything goes past due, and the automatic reminder schedule running underneath.

Billing & QuickBooks

Block liability and pay accrual at a glance. Month-end is one button: the revenue-recognition journal entries and the monthly invoices (T&M, Azure backup per GB, hardware, RMM by device count) post to QBO for review.

Payroll

Hours already sit in QuickBooks as TimeActivity. Click Run Payroll in QBO Payroll; direct deposit works exactly as today.

Reconciliation

Three numbers that must match every night — block liability, receivables, approved hours — against QuickBooks. Red if they don't. This is the bookkeeper's eyeball, automated.

Clients

Balances, device counts, failing checks and past-due amounts per client on one screen, for you and for a tech about to walk on-site.

Getting paid

Due dates are visible everywhere, and reminders send themselves.

Invoice status is read from QuickBooks, so nothing is marked by hand. A reminder cadence runs daily and stops the moment payment posts. The portal turns red at the same time the owner's badge does.

Built to be trusted

The core is small on purpose, and strict on purpose.

Double-entry, append-only ledgerBalances are computed from immutable postings. Mistakes are reversed, never edited. Every number can be rebuilt from history.
Whole numbers onlyHours stored as quarter-hours, money as cents. No rounding drift, ever.
Enforced workflowDraft → Submitted → Approved → Synced → Locked. No self-approval. Locked periods change only by new postings.
Effective-dated ratesClient rates, pay rates and block prices carry valid-from dates. A January price change is one row; old blocks keep their rate.
Idempotent QuickBooks syncA retry can never double-post. QBO can be down for a day and nothing is lost or duplicated.
Reconciliation as a gateLedger vs. QuickBooks vs. N-sight checked nightly; a mismatch blocks month-close until explained.
Full audit logWho approved, who changed a rate, when, before and after.
Operational basicsEntra MFA, secrets in Key Vault, nightly backups with restore drills, monthly CSV export so the data is never trapped.

4Invoicing & receivables

The nightly checks and reports that let the bookkeeper role retire.

After v1, growth is a menu, not a mandate: Teams/text time entry, weekly client digests, Microsoft 365 tenant signals, license resale, project billing, AI-drafted suggestions for techs. See the Possibilities roadmap ↗

Decisions we need

A handful of business rules shape the build. None are hard; all need an owner's call.

When a block runs out mid-entryBill the overage at T&M · overdraft into the next block · hold the entry until a block is sold
When a client is past dueReminders only · reminders + no new block sales · reminders + pause non-emergency support at 30 days
Payment termsNet 30 · Net 15 · due on receipt for blocks, Net 30 monthly
RMM fee structureFlat per client with device count shown · per device · tiered
Who approves timeDrew only · Drew or Mark · auto-approve with weekly review
Block sale accountingLiability recognized as burned (accrual) · revenue at sale (cash basis) — ten-minute question for the CPA
Tech pay compliancePay only on billed hours is piece-rate for W-2 staff; confirm minimum-wage and overtime handling with the payroll advisor. The ledger math is the same either way.
Salaried staff logging timeBurns the client block with no pay effect · salaried staff don't log

All of these can be picked by tapping in the mockup's Decisions section — the choices change how it behaves.
